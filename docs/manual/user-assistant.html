The AI reservation assistant

Every reservation has an AI Assistant tab: a chat that can answer questions about that reservation — what's connected to what, the current config of a device, whether your last apply worked, whether two devices can reach each other. It reads live data with your own permissions and, by default, only ever looks.

The headline

It's read-only out of the box. It can't reserve, cancel, cable, or change a device unless an admin has explicitly turned write tools on — and even then, every change is a dry run you confirm before anything real happens. It only sees what you are already allowed to see.

First things first

Do you even have this feature?

The assistant uses the same AI provider as topology generation, so it's opt-in per deployment. Open any reservation's detail modal and look at the tab row:

You see an AI Assistant tab

It's enabled and you own this reservation. Go ahead.

No AI Assistant tab

Either AI is off on this deployment, or you don't own this reservation. The assistant only ever runs against reservations that are yours.

The provider can be a hosted model or one your team self-hosts. The assistant is the most demanding AI surface in HERD — if your deployment runs a small local model, simple questions will be fine but deep multi-step diagnostics may be weaker than on a large model. That's a deployment choice, not something you set.

Getting there

Opening the assistant

Go to Reservations, click a reservation you own to open its detail modal, then click the AI Assistant tab. It sits alongside Details, Inventory, Routes, and Schedule.

  1. The tab. Only here when AI is configured and the reservation is yours. Everything below is scoped to this one reservation.
  2. The answer. Grounded in live data — device names, real config, actual paths — not a guess. If it can't find something, it says so rather than inventing it.
  3. The tool-call panel. Collapsible. Shows exactly which read-only lookups the assistant made, with arguments and timing, so you can see how it reached the answer.
  4. The input. Type, then Enter to send or click Send.

Talking to it

Asking a question

Type a question (up to 4000 characters) and press Enter to send, or Shift + Enter for a newline. Plain language works; you don't need to know device ids or HERD jargon.

Good things to ask:

  • “What devices are reserved and how are they connected?”
  • “What is the current config of my firewall?”
  • “Did the last apply on switch-a succeed?”
  • “Is there an L2 path between my firewall and my client?”
  • “When does this reservation end?” — answered instantly, with no lookups at all.

How it actually answers

Reading the answer

Behind the scenes the assistant is handed a thin seed when the conversation opens — the reservation's basics and a one-line summary of each device. If your question fits in that, it answers immediately with zero tool calls. If it needs more (a device's real config, the cable path, the last run's result), it calls one or more read-only tools, then answers. The tool-call panel under each answer is your receipt.

The panel is how you trust it

If an answer looks surprising, expand the tool panel. The named lookups and their arguments tell you whether the assistant actually checked the thing it's claiming — or whether it's reasoning from the seed alone.

Its reach is your reach

What the assistant can see

Every lookup the assistant makes runs as you — same login, same device-group visibility, same ownership rules. It can't see a device you can't see, or peek into someone else's reservation. There are seven read-only tools:

ToolWhat it answers
Device detailFields and metadata for one device. Password fields are stripped before the model ever sees them.
Device portsThe device's ports and what each is cabled to.
Current configThe latest config version on a device, plus who applied it and when.
Config schemaWhich config keys are valid for that device's type.
Config historyRecent config versions (metadata only, newest first).
Find pathWhether two devices in this reservation can reach each other, and over how many hops.
Recent executionsThe last driver runs for this reservation — the reservation is pinned server-side, so it can't be redirected at another one.

Keeping the thread going

Follow-ups & new threads

  • Follow-ups remember. Ask “and on switch-b?” after a question about switch-a and it carries the context. The thread shows your questions and its answers in order.
  • It survives a reopen. Close the modal and reopen it in the same browser session and your conversation is still there. A long-idle thread (a day by default) is retired automatically, and your next question quietly starts fresh.
  • Start over anytime. Start new conversation clears the thread and drops the old context, so the assistant isn't anchored on an earlier tangent.

The answer streams in as it is written, so you watch it build word by word. While the assistant reads your devices it shows a short status (for example "running tools") before the reply begins, so a deep question that fans out into several lookups shows progress rather than a blank wait. If it stalls badly it times out with a message rather than hanging.

Beyond looking

Asking it to change config

By default the assistant cannot change anything. If your admin has turned on write tools for the deployment, you can also ask it to propose a config change — but the safety rail is always the same: it builds the change as a dry run first, shows you exactly what the device would receive, and does nothing real until you confirm.

  1. The transcript. The literal commands the driver simulated. This is what a real apply would send, line for line.
  2. Confirm & apply. Your decision, not the model's. Cancel and nothing happened. Drivers that haven't opted into dry-run mode can't be driven this way at all.
No write tools? Then it's looking only

If your deployment hasn't enabled write tools, the assistant simply won't offer to change anything — it'll tell you what it would do and leave the doing to you. There is no way for it to apply a change without the confirm step.

The fine print that's actually reassuring

Privacy & limits

  • Your questions aren't logged. HERD records that a question happened (length, timing, which tools ran) but never the text of what you asked or the data that came back.
  • Passwords never reach the model. Password-typed fields are stripped server-side before any device detail is handed to the assistant.
  • It can't be tricked into other reservations. The reservation is fixed when the chat opens; the executions tool won't accept a different one.
  • There are guardrails on length. Very long answers get trimmed, the assistant caps how many lookups one question can trigger, and a question that's still working after a deadline returns a (possibly partial) answer rather than spinning forever.
The tab is missing, or a question errored out
  • No AI Assistant tab at all: AI isn't configured on this deployment (same switch as the Use AI button) — ask your admin — or you're looking at a reservation you don't own.
  • “Not configured” / it refuses: the AI provider isn't set up. Admin task, not yours.
  • It timed out: a heavy question that fanned out into many lookups can hit the deadline. Ask something narrower, or break it into two questions.
  • It says it can't see a device: that device isn't in your visibility. Same permissions fix as first boot — the assistant is bounded by your access on purpose.
