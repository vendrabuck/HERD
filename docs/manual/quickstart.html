User track · Step 1

First boot & signing in

You just got a link to HERD and a login. This page tells you exactly what you're looking at, gets you signed in, and explains the thing that confuses everyone on day one: why the screen looks empty.

Before you message anyone

HERD is a lab reservation tool. You use it to claim real hardware (switches, firewalls, test servers) for a block of time, optionally draw how it's wired, then run your tests. That's the whole job. If a screen is empty or a button is missing, it's almost always a permissions or setup thing — not you breaking it. The fixes are on this page and in Troubleshooting.

Orientation

The 30-second tour

This is the sign-in screen — the first thing you'll see. Everything you need is in one card.

  1. The wrench (admins only). If HERD has never been configured, an admin uses this to do first-run setup. As a regular user you will never touch it — ignore it.
  2. Email. Your work email, or your directory username if your company wired HERD to its login system. If unsure, try email first.
  3. Sign in. Already have an account? Type your details and click here. Done.
  4. Register. No account yet? Click here first. (Unless it's greyed out — see below.)

Getting in

Signing in

Open the address you were given

Usually something like https://herd.yourcompany.com. For a local test install it's just https://localhost. Your browser may warn about the certificate on a local install — that's expected; continue through it.

Type your email and password, then Sign in

If your company uses LDAP / Active Directory, type your directory username (not necessarily your email) and your normal corporate password. HERD doesn't store that password — it checks it against the directory.

You land on the Reservations page

That's home base. If it's empty, that's normal — jump to the empty-screen section.

A banner about "not configured"?

That message is for administrators — it means the platform's first-run setup hasn't been finished. If the login form is greyed out, you can't fix this yourself: ping whoever installed HERD and point them at the first-day setup guide. Then come back.

No account yet

Registering

Click Register on the sign-in card. You'll get a short form; submit it and you're issued the default user role. That's enough to browse and reserve — admin powers are granted separately by a superadmin.

The Register button is greyed outLDAP / AD shops

If your company connected HERD to its corporate directory, self-registration is turned off on purpose. You don't need it: your HERD account is created automatically the first time you log in with your directory credentials. Just sign in — the account appears behind the scenes.

The thing that confuses everyone

Why everything looks empty

You logged in, clicked Inventory or Topology... and it's blank. Nothing's broken. New accounts start in a group called Not Grouped, and a brand-new account can't see any equipment until an admin grants its group access to some devices.

  1. "No devices found" with a count of (0) means your user group hasn't been granted visibility to any device group yet. This is a one-line fix on the admin side.
The exact thing to ask your admin

"Can you add my user group to the device group(s) for the gear I need to see?" That's the whole request. Once they do it, reload the page and the inventory and equipment palette fill in. Admins: this is covered in first-day setup, step 5.

Getting your bearings

What each tab does

The dark bar across the top is always there. Here's every tab, plain and short.

  1. HERD wordmark — click it any time to get back to home base. Lost? Start here.
  2. Inventory — the catalogue of equipment you're allowed to see. Browse and search here.
  3. Topology — the drag-and-drop canvas where you draw how devices are wired together.
  4. Reservations — your bookings, and the calendar of everyone's bookings. This is where you claim gear.
  5. Reporting admin only — utilization numbers for leadership. The tab is visible to everyone, but opening it as a non-admin gets you redirected away (that's fine).
  6. The bell — notifications about your reservations. The number is your unread count.
