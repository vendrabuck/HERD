Your first device & its cabling

Templates and drivers exist (from first-day setup). Now you'll add a real device, give it ports, and record the physical cabling between ports — the thing that makes topology edges turn green instead of red.

The shape of this

A device has ports. A cable connects one port to another port. HERD's pathfinder uses those cables to decide whether two DUTs can actually talk. No cabling recorded = red edges = no reservations. So this page is what unblocks your users' topologies.

Step one

Add your first device

Go to Inventory → Add device. Pick a template; HERD shows the fields that template defined.

  1. Template. Decides the fields, the driver, and whether the device is exclusive or shared. No template = can't add the device (go back to setup step 2).
  2. Name. What everyone will see in inventory and on the canvas. Keep it recognisable.
  3. Initial group. Optionally drop it straight into a real device group so it's visible immediately.
  4. Add device. It appears in inventory right away.

Step two

Give it ports

A device with no ports can't be cabled. Open the device's detail page and click Add ports. Pick a port template, fill the field data, submit. Bulk creation is supported — add a whole row of ge-0 … ge-47 at once.

Ports are children of a device and use port-type templates (no driver). If you don't have one yet, make it the same way you made the device template — just pick "port" as the type.

Step three

See ports right in the table

Back in Inventory, click the chevron on a row to expand it. You get audit info, group membership, and the device's ports — each with a Connected To link if it's cabled.

  1. The chevron expands and collapses the row's detail.
  2. Connected To. A cabled port shows the device and port on the other end — click through to it.
  3. "not cabled". This port has no physical connection recorded. Edges that try to use it go red with "uncabled port".

The important part

Cabling things up

Cabling is the physical-layer truth HERD reasons over. A connection record says "port A on device A is physically connected to port B on device B." You record one per real cable in your rack. Go to Administration → Connections and click Create Connection.

  1. Device A. Type at least two characters to search, click the match. Picked devices show with a Change link.
  2. Port A. The dropdown unlocks once Device A is chosen, and lists that device's ports.
  3. Connection Type. Free text, defaults to ethernet — label the medium (e.g. fiber, dac) if it helps your records. Notes is optional free text.
  4. Create. The pathfinder picks it up immediately — any topology edge that needed this hop revalidates on the spot.
Cable to the switches, not DUT-to-DUT

DUTs rarely plug straight into each other — they plug into L1/L2 switches that route between them. Record the real cabling (DUT → switch port), and the pathfinder figures out the multi-hop route on its own. That's why a valid topology edge says "3 hops," not "1 cable."

Closing the loop

Verify it went green

The whole point of recording cabling is that users' topology edges turn green. To confirm your work:

Open a topology that uses the devices you just cabled

Or build a quick test one with two DUTs that should now have a path.

Check the edge badges

green + "N hops" means the pathfinder found a route through your cabling. A leftover red means a hop is still missing — trace the cable chain and look for the gap.

Still red after cabling?

Two usual causes: (1) a switch in the middle of the path isn't in inventory yet — add it and its cabling; (2) the two DUTs genuinely sit in physically isolated fabrics. For cross-site links, add a virtual tunnel device cabled into both fabrics. More fixes in Troubleshooting.

Doing it a hundred times

Bulk import & export

Adding devices one at a time is fine for a few. For onboarding a whole fleet — or moving inventory between two HERD instances — HERD round-trips devices, templates, and topologies as CSV or JSON. Import isn't a back door: every row runs through the same validation and permissions as adding one by hand.

Export to get the shape

Export any resource to CSV or JSON. That file is the import format — the easiest way to learn the columns is to export one real example and copy its layout.

Mind the order

References travel by name, not by hidden id (so they survive a move between instances). Import templates before devices (a device names its template) and devices before topologies (a topology names its devices).

Always dry-run first

Run the import as a dry run and HERD validates everything and shows you a per-row report — would create / would update / rejected, with a reason — without writing a thing. Fix the rejects, then run it for real.

  1. The tally. How many rows would be created, updated, or rejected — your go/no-go at a glance.
  2. A rejected row. Isolated and explained (here, a template name that doesn't exist on this instance). Every other row still imports; rejects are rolled back cleanly, leaving no half-written record.

Devices and templates are admin-only to import. Topologies can be exported and imported by any signed-in user. Reservations, permissions, and users are deliberately not bulk-importable — those are created in HERD, not migrated in a spreadsheet.
