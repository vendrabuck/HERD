Notifications & settings

The bell keeps you posted on your reservations — and, if you're an admin or hold a reservation on a sick device, on device health. Out of the box it tells you everything in-app; this page shows you how to route it to email or chat, get a heads-up before a reservation ends, and quiet down the events you don't care about.

Top-right corner

The bell

The bell lives in the header. The red number is your unread count; it refreshes every 30 seconds while you're signed in. Events reach the notification service within milliseconds of the change that caused them; the 30 seconds is just how often the bell asks for a fresh count.

  1. The bell. Ticks up whenever a reservation you own changes state, when one is about to expire, or when a device you're using (or, for admins, any device) goes unhealthy. Click it to open the panel.
  2. Settings. Where you turn the noise up or down. Also reachable from inside the bell panel.

What's in there

The notifications panel

Click the bell to open a panel of your latest 20 notifications.

  1. A notification. Click the dot to mark one read, or use the row controls to delete it.
  2. Mark all read. Clears the unread count in one shot.
  3. Settings. Jumps straight to the notifications settings (same as the header link).

What fires a notification

What sets it off

Three families of event reach the bell:

Reservation lifecycle

Confirmed, activated, updated, completed, cancelled, failed — for reservations you own.

Expiring soon

A single reminder a configurable window before a reservation's end time, so a deadline doesn't sneak up.

Device health

When a device crosses into unhealthy (or recovers). Goes to admins and to anyone with an active reservation on it. See health monitoring.

The expiry reminder fires once per reservation, and health alerts fire on the threshold crossing, not on every failed poll — so a flapping device won't bury you. You can switch any of these off individually below.

Where it lands

Email, chat & webhooks

The bell is always there. On top of it, your deployment may offer three opt-in outbound channels — all off by default, each toggled per-user in settings:

ChannelWhat it does
In-app bellAlways on. The header bell and its panel. Keeps history even if you mute it.
EmailThe same notifications, sent to your address. Good for things you'd miss while not in HERD.
ChatPosts to a Slack-style channel via an incoming webhook your admin configured.
WebhookA signed POST to an endpoint you own — for wiring HERD events into your own automation.

Channels are independent: if email is having a bad day, your bell and chat still fire. Whether a channel is even available (and where chat/webhooks point) is set by your admin at the deployment level — you control the on/off for your account.

Making it quieter (or louder)

Tuning what you get

Settings has two cards: Profile on top (legend item 4) and Notifications under it. In Notifications there are two levels of control: which channels carry notifications (the in-app bell is one of the four toggles, there's no separate master switch), and which of six event types you want at all:

  1. Channels. Four independent toggles: in-app, email, chat, webhook. There's no separate master switch; turning off "Show in-app notifications" silences just the bell (without deleting history), the same way turning off email only stops email. In-app and email are on here; chat and webhook are off, and outbound channels start off until you enable them.
  2. Per-event toggles. Seven individual checkboxes: the six reservation lifecycle events (confirmed, updated, cancelled, completed, expiring soon, failed) plus Device health changes. Here, device-health is unchecked. Anything unchecked stops arriving; missing entries default to on.
  3. Save. Applies to new events immediately — no restart, no reload.
  4. Profile. Your Sign-in email and Username are shown read-only; they are your login identity and no screen changes them. Under them are three fields: Name (required, 1 to 200 characters, need not be unique; "Name is required." appears under it when blank), Contact email (optional; hint "Where email notifications go. Leave blank to use the sign-in email."), and Phone (optional; hint "Stored and shown only. HERD does not call or text this number."; digits, spaces, +, -, (, and ), at least one digit, up to 32 characters). Clearing an optional field and saving removes the value. Save profile stays disabled until a field changed; a save shows the toast "Profile saved", or the server's reason. Email notifications go to the contact email when one is set, else to the sign-in email, and a change takes effect within about 30 seconds. LDAP accounts edit the same three fields; nothing is read from the directory for them. The header shows your name, with your username on hover.

LDAP and local accounts share the same notification prefs — they live in your HERD profile, not in your company directory.

Account basics

Profile & password

  • Your profile holds the basics: sign-in email, username, name, optional contact email and phone, role, and group memberships. You edit the name, contact email, and phone in the Profile card of Settings.
  • Changing your password: HERD has no password change screen or route. LDAP users change it in the company directory; HERD doesn't store one for you. Local accounts ask an admin.
  • Forgot your password? There's no self-serve reset today. Contact an admin.

Your Inventory filters and page-size choices are already saved automatically — no setting needed. Set them once and they stick. The Topologies and Reservations searches, filters, and sorts are saved the same way.
